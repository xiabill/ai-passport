# Vibe Typeless 0.4.1

This release ships the ready-to-run macOS Bridge and the matching ESP32-C3
firmware. **Upgrade both.**

## Important fix: your voice was playing out of the speakers

Audio from the device is meant to go only into the virtual microphone. It was
going to the Mac's speakers instead: the output was set, but macOS's audio
engine follows the system default output and falls back to it whenever the
audio configuration changes, and nothing checked where it had actually gone.

The output is now bound directly and read back. If it is anywhere but the
chosen device, output stops — speech played out loud is worse than none — and
a check every few seconds rebinds it if macOS moves it again.

That bug hid another: a dictation app listening to a desk microphone, which
worked only because it picked the speakers back up. So —

## New: a check that something is listening

A couple of seconds into each take, the app asks macOS who is recording and
from which device. If nothing is listening to your virtual microphone, the top
of the window says who is listening to what, so the fix is obvious.

## App

- **Three pages: keys, devices, general.** The status page became a strip
  under the tabs: each device with its battery, anything needing attention,
  and the last action.
- **Pressing a physical button lights its row** in the key settings.
- **Device battery** shows in the strip, the device list and the menu bar.
- No more duplicated setup guide or power mode; packet counters moved into a
  folded diagnostics section.

## Device screen

- **The status line names the Mac** the device belongs to; its own name moves
  to the footer.
- **Every press answers**: a key that sends rather than records lights its
  card, with the double-click or long-press row brightened.
- The idle hint names the dictation key from your bindings, and below 15% asks
  to be charged.
- Keys without a name of their own show their preset or the key itself.

## Previously in 0.4.0

### The app got simpler

It used to be built around two named input methods, and half of it existed to
keep one of them happy: watching its log for state, re-pressing its shortcut
when a press did not take, rewriting its settings file to pick a microphone,
and a setup step telling you to install it. **All of that is gone.**

The app does one thing now: **send a keystroke when a button is pressed**.
Which application listens for that keystroke is no longer its business, which
is what lets it work with dictation tools it has never heard of.

### Button setup, rebuilt

A gesture answers two questions: which key to send, and how to send it.

- **27 presets** in four groups: text (Return, newline, Esc, Tab, Space),
  editing (copy, paste, cut, undo, redo, select all, delete, save, find),
  windows and apps (switch app, close window, new tab, reload, search), system
  and media (screenshot, play/pause, previous, next, volume, mute). Picking one
  also names the key on the device screen.
- **Or record your own**, including a modifier on its own — Right Option, Fn —
  which is what several dictation tools listen for.
- **Three ways to send**: once, twice, or held. Some apps want a double press,
  others a held key, and only you know which.

Five actions remain: voice input, send key, select all and delete, hand over to
another Mac, and none. Voice input differs from sending a key in one bit: the
device arms its own microphone.

The layout follows the hardware: **one card per physical button, three rows**
for click, double click and long press.

### Also

- **Fixed the device sticking on "transcribing".** It waited to be told the
  text had landed, and that message came from watching one particular app.
  The device cannot know when transcription finishes, so it no longer claims
  to: stopping a take returns to ready.
- Four pages became two, and the setup guide six steps became four.

### Your setup carries over

Shortcuts that used to live in settings move onto the gestures that were
sending them, double press included, and actions that were really just a key
become that key. Nothing needs to be set up again.

## Previously in 0.3.12

- **Fixed two Macs pulling the same device back and forth.** In the previous
  release every connection claimed the device, automatic ones included. A
  device only had to fall free for a moment — the other Mac reconnecting, the
  device rebooting — for the second Mac to take it unasked, and then for the
  first to take it back.

  Only a device you ask for is claimed now. Automatic connections still pick up
  free devices, because a device with no owner follows whoever connects; an
  automatic connection to a busy device is refused, which is the point.

  A device taken by another Mac is also left alone by this one's reconnection
  for five minutes, so the two do not trade it the instant it comes free.
  Clicking "use" in settings overrides that at once.

**Install this on both Macs.** A Mac left on the previous release will still
take the device on an automatic connection.

## Previously in 0.3.11

- **Fixed taking a device from another Mac doing nothing.** The device gave a
  newcomer three seconds to ask for it, and macOS spends four to six just
  discovering the service and its characteristics — so every newcomer was
  dropped before it could speak, which looked like a failure to connect. The
  window is now twenty seconds. The protection is unchanged: a connection that
  never asks is still refused.
- **A handover now says so, on both Macs and on the device.** The device tells
  the Mac it is leaving before cutting the link, so that Mac reports a handover
  rather than a bare disconnect; the Mac taking over says so too, and the
  device screen shows it for five seconds.

## Using two Macs

1. Install this release on both (**older ones will not do** — they still have
   the three-second window).
2. With the device on Mac A, Mac B lists it under **Settings → Devices** as in
   use elsewhere.
3. Click **use** on B; the device moves over within seconds and all three
   screens say what happened.

A device never wanders off on its own; only a click moves it.

## Previously in 0.3.10

- **Fixed "the app is damaged" on any Mac but the build machine.** When the
  build found no stable signing identity — which is the case on CI — it skipped
  signing altogether, so the app shipped with only the linker's signature of
  the executable and no CodeResources beside it. macOS reads that contradiction
  as damage. The build now falls back to an ad-hoc signature over the bundle
  and verifies the result, rather than shipping something that fails its own
  check.

## Installing on another Mac

The app is not notarized, so the first launch is still blocked by macOS. That
is expected, and different from being damaged:

1. Unzip and move `FoloVibeBridge.app` to Applications.
2. **Control-click** it in Finder, choose Open, then Open again in the dialog.
3. If there is no Open option at all, go to **System Settings → Privacy &
   Security**, find the blocked-app notice, and choose Open Anyway.

If it still refuses, clear the download quarantine flag:

```sh
xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/FoloVibeBridge.app
```

## Previously in 0.3.9

- **The device screen was rebuilt.** Four grey cards in a column became a
  hierarchy: link and battery on top, one large element saying what the device
  is doing, the keys, and a quiet footer. Idle shows a ring and a microphone,
  transcribing spins it, disconnected swaps it for a Bluetooth glyph, and
  **talking gives the whole area over to a full-width waveform and a timer**.
  Each key card now states all three of its gestures, not just the click.
- **One Mac can use several devices at once.** Every free Passport in range is
  connected and any of them can be used. When two are talking, the one that
  started first owns the audio, so they never interleave into one garbled
  transcript. Settings shows every device in range and its state.
- **A device changes Macs on purpose.** A connected device keeps advertising —
  slowly, flagged as in use — so another Mac can see it but will not take it on
  its own; a click does that. Anything that connects without asking is dropped
  rather than quietly walking off with the device.
- **Devices have their own names now.** The name came from the vendor prefix of
  the address, so every device called itself `FoloVibe-4C11`. It now comes from
  the low bytes. Expect the name to change after this upgrade.
- **Key labels are yours to write.** Each gesture gets a text field in settings;
  the device follows immediately, and blank restores the built-in name.
  Characters the device font never shipped with work too: the Mac draws the
  label and sends the picture.
- **Fixed "charging" sticking after the cable was pulled.** The gauge holds its
  percentage flat once current stops, and the old rule waited for a fall. A
  fading rise now ends it, and the voltage drop of unplugging ends it at once.

## Upgrading

From 0.3.4 or later no cable is needed: "upgrade and restart" in settings, then
"upgrade firmware". **With two devices connected, both are upgraded in turn.**

## Previously in 0.3.8

- **Fixed the app vanishing after an upgrade.** "Upgrade and restart" installed
  the new version correctly but never reopened it — gone from the menu bar and
  the Dock, as though it had been deleted. The helper doing the swap ran as a
  child of the app, so quitting killed it partway through: after the bundle was
  replaced, before the relaunch. It now runs detached, and the whole sequence
  logs to `~/Library/Logs/folovibe-update.log`.
- **The device shows its firmware version.** At the end of the footer that was
  already there, in the same dim colour. A hand-flashed build with uncommitted
  changes gets a star — `0.3.7*` — so it is never mistaken for a release.
- **The device shows when it is charging.** The battery reading turns green with
  a plus sign (`+53%`), and the footer estimates the time to full.
  Above 92% the estimate is withheld: the charger tapers there and a linear
  guess would promise more than it can keep.

  There is no charge-detect line on the board, so this is inferred from the
  direction the fuel gauge is moving. It takes 20 to 40 seconds to appear, and
  it works with a power bank or a wall charger, not just a computer.

## Previously in 0.3.7

- **Fixed the device disappearing while plugged in.** Preparing for deep sleep
  takes the radio down first, and deep sleep normally reboots the chip, so that
  state was cleared by the restart. An attached USB host makes the chip refuse
  to sleep: the device keeps running and the radio was never put back, so it
  stopped advertising and nothing could find it. A refused sleep now restores
  the radio.

## Previously in 0.3.6

- **Putting the device down no longer drops the link.** Blanking the screen
  after five idle minutes disconnected the Mac, so walking away for a coffee
  was enough to lose it. A second timer then took over: sixty seconds
  unconnected meant deep sleep, so waking it by hand did not help — it was
  asleep again before the app finished scanning. Screen-off now only blanks the
  display; the radio keeps working until the deep-sleep deadline, and the
  unconnected window is five minutes, long enough to reconnect.
- **Fixed the app getting stuck on "connecting".** When the stored connection
  record was stale — the device had rebooted, say — the callback never arrived,
  and with no timeout the app neither scanned nor retried nor logged anything.
  Quitting it was the only way out. Every attempt now has an eight-second
  deadline.
- **A hotkey can be a single or a double tap, your choice.** Typeless was
  hardwired to one press and Doubao to two. Which one an input method expects
  is not detectable and changes between versions, so it is now per input
  method. The defaults reproduce the old behaviour.

## Previously in 0.3.5

- **Any shortcut can be bound to any gesture.** The action list used to be a
  fixed menu, so anything outside it could not be mapped at all. Pick "custom
  key", press the combination in the recorder, and it is bound — `⌘C`, `F5`,
  `⌃⌥⇧⌘K` all work. All nine gestures (click, double, long on each of the three
  buttons) can be bound separately.
- **Fixed the link dropping during long speech.** Speaking for a long stretch
  eventually disconnected the device. The audio buffer pool was running dry:
  every 20ms packet takes a buffer, a slightly slow acknowledgement exhausts the
  pool, and the stack reports that as a link failure. The pool is now three
  times larger.
- **Added an ultra power mode.** Screen off after 30 seconds, deep sleep after
  two minutes, for leaving the device on the desk. It also fixes a leak in the
  existing modes: deep sleep left the panel and the fuel gauge drawing current,
  and both are now powered down.

## Previously in 0.3.4

- **The Mac app updates itself.** It checks for a release on launch; press
  "upgrade and restart" in settings and it installs and relaunches. Permissions
  are preserved across the upgrade.
- **Firmware updates from the app.** Settings shows the version running on the
  device; when a newer one exists, one click streams it over Bluetooth in about
  a minute or two. A failed transfer is harmless: the image only becomes
  bootable after it verifies, so the device keeps running what it has.
- **Fixed the other half of the reconnection problem.** The previous release
  fixed the device-side teardown; this one fixes macOS silently restoring an old
  connection, which stopped the device advertising while the app kept scanning
  for it. The app now takes such connections over.

## Previously in 0.3.3

- **Fixed slow or stuck reconnection after sleep.** The device only *started*
  the disconnect before cutting power, so the teardown never reached the Mac.
  CoreBluetooth kept holding a connection to a device that no longer existed
  and ignored the advertisements sent after wake-up until its own supervision
  timeout expired, which is why the device could seem unreachable until it was
  power-cycled. It now waits for the disconnect to be confirmed, so the Mac
  learns the device has gone and reconnects normally on the next wake.

## Previously in 0.3.2

- **The middle key inserts a newline.** A double click or long press sends
  Option+Return, adding a line break without submitting, so writing a
  multi-line message never moves your hand.
- **Keys follow the thumb.** Doubao on the up key, confirm in the middle,
  Typeless on the down key.
- **Bindings are stored on the device.** After a power cycle the keys describe
  themselves immediately instead of waiting for the bridge.
- **A denser interface.** Bindings are edited in a 3x3 grid that fits on one
  screen, spacing and type are tightened throughout, and the window minimum is
  880x600 so a narrow window reflows instead of breaking.
- **Permissions survive upgrades.** `create-signing-identity.sh` sets up a stable
  signing identity; macOS no longer treats each build as a different app. Run it
  once, grant permissions one final time, and they persist.
- **Reworked device screen.** Keys are tinted by action, the waveform actually
  moves while you speak, and the debug lines are gone.
- **Serial screen capture.** Implements the protocol the community publisher
  requires for a live screenshot.

## Previously in 0.3.1

- **Doubao can finally be triggered.** Its hands-free mode requires a double
  click, and the Bridge sent a single press with no hold between down and up,
  which Doubao ignored. Presses now follow the timing of a physical double
  click: press, hold, release, pause, repeat.
- **Default bindings follow the thumb.** Doubao moved to the up key, confirm
  (Return) to the middle key, and Typeless to the down key. Typeless used to sit
  in the middle with confirm below it, which made the hand reach across.
- **On-screen key hints are ordered up, middle, down**, matching the physical
  keys; they used to read middle, down, up.
- **Fixed the missing glyphs.** The font is a subset generated from the Chinese
  characters found in the sources, and the newly added ones rendered as boxes.
- **The mascot no longer drifts upward.** An interrupted jump left it mid-air and
  the next jump started from there, shifting it up a few pixels every time. It
  now always jumps from its standing baseline.
- **One input method can no longer cut off the other.** Pressing the Doubao key
  while Typeless is recording is ignored instead of ending the take. Typeless
  Dictate, Translation, and Ask anything still switch between each other.

## Previously in 0.3.0

- **Fixed the swapped middle and down keys.** The BSP voltage windows did not
  line up with the button enum, so the middle key ran the down key's action and
  vice versa. The up key was unaffected.
- **Every click, double click, and long press is now bindable.** Nine gestures
  across the up, middle, and down keys, all configurable under Settings ->

  requires a reflash.
- **Return no longer races the transcript.** Press the send key after a long
  dictation and Return now waits until the text has landed in the field.
- **The audio device is paired automatically.** The Bridge intersects the
  system's loopback devices with the ones Typeless can actually enumerate and
  picks one both sides can use, instead of assuming BlackHole. At launch, if its

- **Reworked interface.** The sidebar became a top tab bar, the layout no longer

## Download

Open the [latest GitHub Release](https://github.com/xiabill/ai-passport/releases/latest)
and download:

- `FoloVibeBridge-macos.zip`: the macOS companion app.
- `FoloToy-AI-Passport-full.bin`: the merged AI Passport firmware.

The Bridge requires macOS 13 or newer. It is not notarized; if macOS blocks the
first launch, Control-click the app in Finder, choose Open, and confirm.

## Install and set up the Bridge

1. Unzip `FoloVibeBridge-macos.zip` and move `FoloVibeBridge.app` to `/Applications`.
2. Open it and work through the setup guide on the status page, pressing the
   one-click setup button on anything that is blocked.
3. Grant Bluetooth, Accessibility, and Input Monitoring to `FoloVibe Bridge` in
   System Settings -> Privacy & Security.
4. Install a virtual loopback audio device (BlackHole 2ch is the usual choice)
   and select it as the microphone inside Typeless. Then run one-click setup in
   the Bridge and it will align itself to the same device.
5. Pick the same base key in the Bridge that Typeless uses (`Fn` or `F19`). For
   Doubao, pick the key its hands-free mode uses; the default is Right Option.

If an installed loopback device never appears in the Typeless microphone list,
Typeless (Electron) cannot enumerate it — choose one it can see. One-click setup
only ever picks a device that works on both sides.

A Passport accepts one Mac at a time. To switch, release the device on the
current Mac and let the Bridge on the other Mac connect.

## Button actions

Each of the three buttons reports a click, a double click and a long press:
nine gestures, each configured on its own.

A gesture answers two questions: which key to send, and how to send it.

| Action | Behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| Voice input | sends your key **and arms the device's microphone**; press again to stop |
| Send key | sends the key, no recording |
| Select all and delete | two steps, for wiping a take that came out wrong |
| Hand over to another Mac | gives the device to a different Mac |
| None | the gesture does nothing |

**Presets** — 27 of them, and picking one also names the key on the device:
text (Return, newline, Esc, Tab, Space), editing (copy, paste, cut, undo,
redo, select all, delete, save, find), windows and apps (switch app, close
window, new tab, reload, search), system and media (screenshot, play/pause,
previous, next, volume, mute).

**Or record your own**: `⌘C`, `F5`, `⌃⌥⇧⌘K`, and modifiers on their own —
Right Option, Fn — which is what several dictation tools listen for.

**Three ways to send**: once, twice, or held. Pick what the receiving app
expects; clicking an existing key changes it without re-recording.

Each gesture also has a field for what the device screen should call it.
Characters the device font never shipped with work too, because the Mac draws
the label and sends the picture.

## Several devices

Every free Passport in range is connected automatically, and any of them can be
used. Settings lists each one with its state: free, in use here, or in use on
another Mac.

A device another Mac is using is never taken automatically; clicking "use"
does that. The same holds in reverse, so two Macs never trade it back and
forth.

When two devices are talking, the one that started first owns the audio path
and the other is ignored, so the two never interleave.

## Audio behaviour

Microphone audio travels over BLE to the Mac and is written into the loopback
audio device for the input method. The connect, record start, record stop, edit,
and send cues are generated on the Passport itself and played through its I2S ->
ES8311 -> speaker path; they are never substituted by the Mac speaker.

## Flashing the firmware

Only flash the merged firmware from this release. Use the
[web flasher](https://ai-passport.folotoy.cn/tools/web-flasher/) or ESP-IDF
tooling. The image is written from `0x0`:

```bash
python -m esptool --chip esp32c3 \
  -p /dev/cu.usbmodemXXXX -b 460800 \
  write_flash 0x0 FoloToy-AI-Passport-full.bin
```

Never run `erase-flash` on a provisioned device. The merged image preserves the
device identity and the Recovery partition. Reconnect the Bridge afterwards and
wait for the status page to report the device as ready.

## Troubleshooting

- **No button does anything:** the firmware and Bridge versions do not match;
  update both to this release.
- **Middle and down keys are swapped:** fixed since 0.3.0; firmware older than
  that still has the fault.
- **Doubao does not respond:** check that its hands-free mode has a key assigned
  and that it matches the Doubao shortcut in the Bridge. Bridges older than 0.3.1
  could not trigger it at all.
- **No hardware cues:** reconnect and test the connect cue first; if it stays
  silent, check the Bridge log and the device serial log for the ES8311/I2S path.
- **Typeless hears nothing:** make sure the Typeless microphone and the Bridge
  output device are the same one — the status page reports whether they are
  aligned — and that the Bridge has Bluetooth permission.
- **Device not found:** wake the Passport, confirm it advertises as `FoloVibe-*`,
  and keep only one Bridge connected while testing.

The full BLE contract, power rules, development builds, and Recovery constraints
are in the [development guide](../development/vibe-typeless.en.md).
